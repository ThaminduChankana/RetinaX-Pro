CNV (Choroidal Neovascularization)

CNV involves the proliferation of abnormal blood vessels from the choroid into the sub-retinal space, often triggered by inflammation or ischemia. These vessels are fragile and prone to leakage, causing rapid structural disruption. RetinaX Pro identifies these as hyper-reflective signals within the outer retinal layers, providing a pixel-level density assessment for pathological validation.

Critical Path

DME (Diabetic Macular Edema)

DME is characterized by macular thickening due to blood-retinal barrier breakdown. Fluid accumulates within intraretinal cystic spaces, leading to significant visual distortion. Professional grading relies on foveal thickness metrics and the presence of neurosensory detachment signals. Our system segmentation allows for the precise calculation of fluid volume and total retinal burden.

Active Fluid

DR (Diabetic Retinopathy)

DR is the clinical result of chronic hyperglycemia damaging retinal microvasculature. Early detection of microaneurysms and hemorrhages is crucial to prevent proliferative progression. RetinaX Pro's neural layers focus on identifying these subtle vascular lesions within the inner retinal stack, ensuring early intervention before permanent sight impact occurs.

Vascular Risk

ARMD (Age-Related Macular Degeneration)

ARMD is primarily identified by the presence of drusen—nodular deposits of sub-RPE debris. Over time, these lead to RPE atrophy and potential geographic progression. Our AI system segments these focal elevations to calculate total drusen volume and early-stage risk scores, providing a longitudinal view of disease stability and progression.

Monitoring Baseline

MH (Macular Hole)

A Macular Hole is a full-thickness defect in the foveal tissue, triggered by anomalous vitreoretinal traction. This causes immediate disruption to central vision and requires rapid surgical intervention. The Pro pipeline identifies the distinct "break" signal and surrounding intraretinal edema, ensuring a prioritized alert for acute anatomical disruption.

Acute Break

CSR (Central Serous Chorioretinopathy)

CSR involves the accumulation of transparent subretinal fluid, leading to a localized serous detachment. It is typically identified on B-scans as a clear anatomical "bubble" under the fovea. RetinaX Pro's hybrid model distinguishes this from inflammatory fluid by its unique morphology and provides a precise measurement of the serous elevation height.

Localized Serous

Normal (Healthy Retina)

A healthy retina displays a distinct foveal depression and clearly defined anatomical layers. The absence of any hyper-reflective or fluid-based signals is the gold standard for diagnostic normal classification. RetinaX Pro validates this baseline against millions of healthy datasets, ensuring the system remains calibrated for the highest diagnostic specificity.

Optimal Signal

Glaucoma

Glaucoma is a progressive optic neuropathy characterized by an increased cup-to-disc ratio and distinct thinning of the retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL), often associated with elevated intraocular pressure. Using fundus topography analysis, the Pro subsystem maps morphological changes in the optic nerve head, enabling early risk stratification before irreversible peripheral vision loss occurs.

Optic Nerve

Cataract

A Cataract presents as progressive clouding and opacification of the crystalline lens, significantly reducing optical clarity and contrast. In automated fundus analysis, cataracts manifest as generalized image haziness, reduced signal-to-noise ratios, and distinct scattering artifacts. RetinaX identifies this opacification profile during initial image quality gating, classifying it as a distinct structural disorder impacting visual acuity.

Lens Opacity

Hypertensive Retinopathy (HTN-R)

Hypertensive Retinopathy reflects systemic vascular damage appearing directly within the retinal vasculature due to chronic hypertension. Pathological signs include generalized arteriolar narrowing, AV nicking, flame hemorrhages, and in severe cases, macular star formations. The AI pipeline analyzes these branching vascular topologies across fundus images to grade severity and systemic vascular risk.

Systemic Vascular

Image Quality & Artifacts

Diagnostic accuracy depends on scan quality. Motion artifacts, shadowing from floaters, and signal loss can mimic pathology across both OCT and Fundus modalities. Users should ensure clear signal-to-noise ratios and proper anatomical centering (fovea or optic disc) for reliable AI-driven pathological justifications. Identifying these "noise" factors is as crucial as identifying the disease itself.
